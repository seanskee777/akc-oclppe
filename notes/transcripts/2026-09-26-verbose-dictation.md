## Why this file exists separately from the transcript

Because the argument in here is the reason the project exists, and it is not
visible anywhere else.

## The part that changed a mind

The objection to the residual model was always: *a ledger proves usage, but it
cannot compel a company to pay.* That is legally true and it remains true.

What the dictation adds is the constituency. The people whose ideas get scraped
into an unusable pile are the people already inside the platform economy and
already getting nothing. The ones a transparent tether would actually reach are
the ones outside it — grassroots artists, small developers, people who share
because they want to be found. For that group, being *visible* is worth more
than being *paid*, and visibility is exactly what a public, verifiable record
produces and a private contract does not.

So the honest formulation is not "you will get paid." It is **"you will not be
invisible."** The money is the part that may never arrive. The proof that the
idea existed, and who it belonged to, is the part that will.

Open source runs on the same mechanism and nobody calls it unenforceable.
Red Hat, Sentry, GitHub Sponsors — none of it is compelled. It works anyway,
for people who care, at a scale that a legal remedy could never reach.

## The part that is not a ledger at all

> "It's not a ledger. It's just an area for people to exist. Will they sell it or
> get paid for it? Who knows?"

That sentence is the actual thesis. The payment is unresolved and was never the
load-bearing part. The thing being built is a place where a person can be
present and attributable, without a gatekeeper deciding who is allowed to be.

## Other threads in the same recording

These are recorded so they are not lost. **THINK S.O.** is discussed at length

has notes of its own.

### The provenance rule this project is named for

> "A parallel system should always respect where the branch it came from. Because
> if something goes down, the parallel system might not ever work. Nobody's
> promising the branches. It's just a node. It's just a mesh."

This is why the global agent rules require a separate phrase to amend them. The
rule change is not a formality; it is the architecture restated.

### On whether the thing has to work

> "Will it work? I don't know, it doesn't have to. Does it have to? Does it work
> for me? Hopefully."

Building for one person is a complete answer. The "does it scale" critique
answers a question nobody asked.

### On the fire extinguisher

> "You don't have to know that Rome always burns in a day. Why? Because they
> didn't think enough to have a fucking fire extinguisher."

The signed log is the extinguisher. The tree burning is not the interesting
failure mode; having nothing to point at afterwards is.
